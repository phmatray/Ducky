## Grammar: interrogation transcript

A new grammar, not one of the eight defined ones. A section is a question put to the library.

- **Navigation:** a docket of the duck's questions in a sticky rail. Each question lights its lamp once answered.
- **Sequence:** a readable transcript in normal document flow, one silent question, one pinned demonstration, then ruled reference sections.
- **Ending:** the install command, on the ground the page started on.
- **Bans:** no pinned reading (long answers always scroll normally), no scrims, no shadows, no gradients, no tints, no drift, no scrub video, no magnet or spotlight, no invented numbers.

Why the eight lost: filmic, continuous world and cutlist hide or rush code that visitors need to read. Live surface bans the display headings this identity depends on. Chaptered editorial has no one to ask the questions. Poster, gallery and split stage fit neither the proof list nor the argument.

## The peak

"I scrolled a race between two requests, and the late answer got stopped at the door. When I scrolled back, it un-happened."

It lives in the race act, which holds the largest scroll span on the page (6 viewport-heights). The yellow question before it is the authored silence, so the harness may report that screen as sparse.

**It's the site where** your scroll runs a race between two requests and you watch the stale one get refused.

## Signature move

The scroll position is the clock of a small reducer. Every frame recomputes the state from the event log up to that moment, so the log, the lamps, the state readout and the drop are all a pure function of scroll. The page labels it as a JavaScript model, and the forecast values are demonstration values.
